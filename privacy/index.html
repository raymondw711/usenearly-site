1. What the app does

It alerts you when a place matching what you chose is nearby. It doesn't know your schedule or your plans, and it doesn't ask.

2. Location

Your position is worked out on your iPhone by iOS, and matching runs on the device. If you allow “Always”, the app uses it to alert you while it's closed. We do not keep a location history. A rough area reaches our server only when Nearly searches for places: about 100 m for a walk, about 1 km for a drive, and about 1 km when it looks for places by your own words (see “Finding places by your words” and “Nearly search” below). Your exact location stays on your iPhone.

To search for places, the app uses Apple Maps on your iPhone (a category and a map region; Apple's privacy policy covers that). When you type an address, such as your home, Apple Maps receives what you type so it can suggest matching addresses. To decide how strict the rating bar should be, the app also asks Apple Maps what kind of area you're in (for example a big city or a small town), which sends your approximate position to Apple. We don't receive it.

Ratings, opening hours and photos (Google Maps)

Apple Maps doesn't give the app ratings or opening hours. With Nearly Plus, the app asks Google Maps for a place's rating, opening hours and one photo when you open that place. If a watch has a minimum Google rating (a Plus setting), the app also checks the rating just before an alert, and no alert is sent if the rating is too low or can't be checked. On the free plan the app makes no Google requests from your iPhone. Nothing is looked up while the app searches in the background or while you look at the map or the list.

For each of those look-ups, your iPhone contacts Google directly. It first sends the place's name and the place's own map position, as Apple Maps gives them, so Google can find the same place (this step is skipped when the app already knows the Google ID). It then asks for that place's rating, number of ratings, opening hours and a photo, which is shown with its author's name. The app does not send Google your own position, your location history, what you watch for, your taste words, or any account or device ID. Like any request from your phone, Google sees your IP address. For those look-ups, we don't receive the request or the answer on our servers.

Because the place is near you when the alert fires or when you open it, a look-up shows roughly where you were at that moment. Google handles these requests under the Google Privacy Policy.

For places already on your list, the app may also send our server the place's name and map position (not your exact location). Our server asks Google Maps for opening hours and whether the place is open or closed, and may read the place's own website for hours. We keep that answer as section 6 explains.

What the app keeps on your iPhone: the Google ID for each place it has looked up (so it doesn't have to match the place again) and a count of look-ups this month. The rating, hours and photo are kept in memory while the app is running and are never saved to your iPhone's storage. “Delete all data on this iPhone” removes the IDs and the count.

Nearly Plus includes about 40 look-ups a month for places you open. When they're used up, or Google doesn't answer, the app shows Apple Maps information only. Permanently or temporarily closed places never show. Places Google Maps can't match never show or alert. For some categories the app also drops places whose Google types don't fit the watch.

Finding places by your words (Google Maps)

With Nearly Plus, if you've switched on Allow AI checks, Nearly can look for places Apple Maps didn't list. For a watch with your own words, the app sends our server those words, the category, a rough area (about 1 km) and how far you said you'd go. Our server asks Google Maps for matching places nearby (their name, position, address and website). It keeps Google's answer for 24 hours under that rounded area, with nothing about you, so the same search nearby doesn't cost again, and it keeps a count of your searches (time and outcome, no position) to apply your monthly limit. The app then checks the nearest few places as described under AI checks in section 4, and shows places found this way marked “Found with Google Maps”.

Nearly search

With Nearly Plus, if you've switched on Allow AI checks, a watch can also search for places around you. Your iPhone rounds your position (to about 100 m for a walk, about 1 km for a drive) and sends our server your words, the category, that rounded area and how far you said you'd go. Our server asks Google Maps for nearby places and receives each place's name, position, address, website, listing summary and a few review snippets. It then sends OpenAI your words, each place's name, address, listing details and review snippets, the place's website address, text from that site when it can be read, and roughly how far the place is from the rounded area. Your name, account and exact location are not sent to OpenAI, and OpenAI is asked not to store the request. If Google Maps doesn't return places to check, our server may send OpenAI the same rounded area — still not your exact location — and ask it to search the web instead. Places found this way are marked “Found with Google Maps”. We keep the shared answer as section 6 explains.

3. What stays on your iPhone

The categories you watch for and the taste choices and words you pick, how far you'll walk, your alert history, places you've hidden, Passport stamps and badge progress, and your Appearance choice (System, Light or Dark), quiet hours, Quiet for today and Same place again settings. Watches follow you as you move. The app doesn't ask for a home, work or other address. There is no daily alert limit to set.

My places also stays on your iPhone: places you save or mark visited, the date, and any note you add. We never receive it.

All of this is kept in the app's storage, protected by iOS data protection. None of it is copied to our server, except as section 4 describes.

Sign in with Apple is required. The app keeps, in the iPhone's Keychain on this device only, the ID number Apple gives us for you and the sign-in session for our server. The app doesn't ask Apple for your name or email.

Passport stamps sync automatically through your own iCloud whenever iCloud is available for Nearly. Settings has no backup status text; failures stay in diagnostics. Each backed-up stamp has the place's name, date, kind and a rough position (about 100 m). Stamps never go to Nearly's server.

4. What leaves your iPhone

  • Sign in with Apple (required). When you sign in, the app sends Apple's sign-in token to our server. Our server checks it with Apple and keeps an account record: the ID number Apple gives us for you, when the account was created and when it was last used, and sign-in session records (stored in scrambled form, not readable). No name or email is requested or kept.
  • Plus purchases. After the app refreshes your purchases with Apple, it sends Apple's signed purchase records to our server so your Plus subscription stays in step with your account. Our server keeps, linked to your account: the transaction ID, the product, purchase and expiry dates, its status, whether it was shared through Family Sharing, and whether it was a test purchase. We never receive your payment details.
  • Ratings, hours and photo look-ups (Plus). Sent from your iPhone straight to Google Maps when you open a place, or just before an alert if the watch has a minimum rating, as described in section 2. Our server isn't involved in those look-ups. Google’s Privacy Policy applies to those requests.
  • Opening hours and open-or-closed status. For places on your list, the app may send our server the place's name and map position (not your exact location). Our server asks Google Maps and may also read the place's own website for hours, as described in section 2.
  • Website previews. When you open a place that has a website, your iPhone loads that website's public page directly to show its preview image, with a credit to the site. This is an ordinary web request from your phone, so that website sees your IP address, as it would if you visited it. We don't send it anything about you, and the image is kept in memory only while the app is open. We don't receive or store it.
  • Taste words. Apart from AI checks (below), taste checks on our server are switched off in the current app, so your taste words aren't sent. If we switch them on, the app will send the taste words with the kind of place and the IDs of nearby places Apple Maps found, without your account, a device ID or your position, and our server will keep only a count of these searches with the kind of place and a scrambled (hashed) form of the taste words. We'll update this policy before that happens.
  • AI checks (Plus). Only if you're signed in with Plus and have switched on Allow AI checks, the app sends our server what you asked for, whether you typed it or picked a built-in choice (for example “pour over”), the category, the place's Apple Maps ID, name and website, and how far the place is from you compared with your walk. Our server reads that place's public website and sends what you asked for, the category, the place's name and the website text to OpenAI to check it. Your account, position and device are not sent to OpenAI, and OpenAI is asked not to store the request. Our server keeps a record of each check with your account (time and result) to apply your monthly limit, and a shared answer for that place and request with nothing about you.
  • Finding places by your words (Plus). Under the same conditions as AI checks, the app sends our server your words, the category, a rough area (about 1 km) and your walk or drive distance. Our server asks Google Maps as described in section 2, and doesn't store your exact position.
  • Nearly search (Plus). Under the same conditions as AI checks, your iPhone rounds your position (to about 100 m for a walk, about 1 km for a drive) and sends it with your words, the category and your walk or drive distance to our server, which asks Google Maps and OpenAI as described in section 2. Your exact location is not sent or stored.
  • Usage totals. The current app doesn't show a usage totals switch and sends no totals. If we add them, totals would be daily counts, such as the number of alerts that day, with no user, device, IP or location field. In the current app nothing is sent yet.

Alerts are created on your iPhone. We don't use a push service or a push token.

The app may also fetch short in-app status, tip and badge wording from our server, with a copy bundled in the app if the fetch fails. That response is wording only and does not include your watches, places or location.

5. What we don't do

No ads. We don't sell or share data for advertising, track you across apps, or use data brokers. We don't store raw IP addresses.

6. Retention

Data on your iPhone stays until you delete it or delete the app. On our server:

  • Your account record and the purchase records linked to it are kept until you delete your account.
  • Sign-in sessions expire after 60 days without use and are then removed.
  • Counts of searches, look-ups and AI checks (time, cost and outcome, with no words or position) are kept for 25 months so we can apply limits. When you delete your account, those rows are kept only as unnamed counts for our spending limits.
  • A shared answer for a Nearly search is kept under the words and the rounded area, not your account, for 12 hours when every criterion is decided, 4 hours when some stay unconfirmed, and 1 hour when the search found nothing usable.
  • Google's list of places for a search by your words is kept 24 hours under the rounded area (about 1 km), with nothing about you.
  • A check of one place against what you asked for (an AI check) is kept as a shared answer for 21 days, or 24 hours if that place's website couldn't be reached. The shared answer has nothing about you.
  • A confirmed finding that a place meets a requirement (the place, the requirement, a short evidence quote and the source page, and when it was checked) is kept for 3 days, with no account, location or watch name, and is removed daily once it expires.
  • A settled judgment of one place for one exact set of criteria is reused for 3 days.
  • A confirmed match is held for 7 days so a later search doesn't drop it if a re-check is unclear.
  • Open or closed status and hours from Google are kept 7 days if the place is open or temporarily closed, 30 days if closed for good, and 6 hours if Google couldn't match the place. Hours read from the place's own website are kept 7 days, or 24 hours if no hours were found.

How long Google and OpenAI keep request data is set by their own policies.

7. Your choices

You can turn off location or notifications at any time. In Settings › Your data you can export everything the app keeps on your iPhone, including My places, as a file. “Delete all data on this iPhone” in the same place wipes your watches, taste words, alert history, My places and saved Google data, and stops watching. Deleting the app also removes it. Tap Sign out in Settings › Account to remove the Apple ID number and the sign-in session from this iPhone; this doesn't delete your account on our server. To delete your account and the records our server keeps with it, go to Settings › Account › Delete account in the app. This deletes your account and the purchase records linked to it on our server, and everything Nearly keeps on this iPhone. You can also stop using Sign in with Apple for this app in your iPhone's Apple Account settings. To stop Passport stamp backup, turn off iCloud for Nearly in the iPhone's Settings (the app has no backup toggle). Neither “Delete all data on this iPhone” nor deleting the app removes your account on our server.

8. Children

The app is not directed to children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their data.
